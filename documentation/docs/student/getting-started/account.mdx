This page explains two things about your account that can surprise you: how you change your email address, and why
Artemis sometimes asks you to sign in again before you can add an SSH key, a passkey or an access token.

## Changing your email address

Your email address is where Artemis sends the link to reset your password. Whoever controls it can take over your
account, so Artemis changes it only after you have confirmed the change **from both addresses**: the one you have now and
the new one.

1. Open your user menu in the navigation bar, choose **User Settings**, open **Account Information** and click
   **Edit Account Settings**.
2. Enter the new address in **Email** and click **Save**.
3. Artemis sends two emails. "Confirm the change of your Artemis e-mail address" goes to your current address, and
   "Confirm your new Artemis e-mail address" goes to the new one.
4. Open the link in each email and click **Confirm email change** on the page that opens. The page confirms only after
   you click, so a mail program that opens links on your behalf cannot confirm a change for you.
5. Once both addresses are confirmed, your address changes. Your previous address receives a short notice.

Until then you keep using your current address. The settings page shows **Email change waiting for confirmation**, which
of the two addresses is **confirmed** and which is **not confirmed yet**, and until when the links are valid. They are
valid for 24 hours.

### While a change is waiting

- **Resend confirmation emails** sends new links to both addresses. The earlier links stop working, and what you had
  confirmed so far has to be confirmed again.
- **Cancel change** withdraws the request. Your address stays as it is.
- You can request a change, or resend the emails, once per minute.
- A request ends on its own when your password is changed or reset, and when an administrator changes your password or
  your address.
- If you can no longer open the mailbox of your current address, ask an administrator to change the address for you.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  If you receive a confirmation email that you did not ask for, do not follow its link. Someone else may be using your
  account. Change your password, which also cancels the request.

</Callout>

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Some installations cannot send email. There, the **Email** field is disabled and the page tells you to ask an
  administrator. If your institution's login system manages your account, your address comes from there and follows it.

</Callout>

## Signing in again before you add a key, passkey or token

When your password has been changed or reset, a browser that was still signed in from before can keep reading Artemis,
but it can no longer add anything that would outlive the password change. That covers SSH keys, passkeys and access
tokens, and it includes showing you the access token of an exercise repository for cloning.

Without this, someone using a forgotten session could add their own key or passkey, and it would keep working after you
changed your password.

What you see: Artemis signs you out and opens the sign-in page, saying that you signed in before your credentials last
changed. After you sign in again, you land on the page you came from and can repeat what you were doing.

This also applies to you right after you change your own password: sign in again before you add a new SSH key or
passkey.

See [Integrated Code Lifecycle](/student/tools-reference/integrated-code-lifecycle) for how to use SSH keys and access
tokens.
