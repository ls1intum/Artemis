Global search finds what you are looking for across all of your courses from anywhere in Artemis: an exercise, a
lecture, the slide that explains a topic, an FAQ or a channel. If you ask a question instead of typing a name, Iris can
answer it from your course material, with each statement linked to where it comes from.

## Opening the Search

Click **Search** in the navigation bar, or press **Cmd+K** on macOS and **Ctrl+K** on Windows and Linux. The same
shortcut closes it again, as does **Esc**. You can start typing right away: anything you type while the search opens
goes into the search field.

Where you open it decides where it starts:

- **Outside a course**, it starts with the filter picker, which offers **Filter by type**, **Scope to a course** and
  **Exclude content**. You can ignore it and type; typing leaves the picker and searches.
- **Inside a course**, it starts scoped to that course: a chip with the course's name sits under the search field and
  the course's content is listed right away. Remove the chip to search all of your courses.

The search starts once you have typed at least three characters. For a very short term, Artemis tells you that a
longer one gives better results.

## What You Can Find

The search covers courses, exercises, lectures and lecture units, exam overviews, FAQs and channels, in every course you
are enrolled in, including courses that have already ended. Results from running courses rank above those from ended
courses, although a much better match from an ended course can still come first. When the same item exists in several
semesters of a course, it appears once, from the best-ranked course.

Each result shows its title, its kind, its course and, depending on the kind, its due date and points. Selecting a
result opens it, just as clicking it in the course would. **Show more results** at the end of the list loads more.

{/* SCREENSHOT results.png: a search for a topic that matches an exercise, a lecture unit and an FAQ in different courses.
    Show at least one exercise row with its due date and points, and the Slides & videos row above the results.
    Caption: "Results from several courses". */}

You only find what you could also open in the course itself:

- exercises once they are released, and exam exercises once the exam has started;
- lecture units once their release date has passed;
- FAQs once they are accepted;
- exams you are registered for, and test exams once they are visible;
- public and course-wide channels, and the channel of an exercise or exam only once that exercise is released or the exam is visible.

Messages and replies are not part of global search. To search messages, use the search in the course
[communication](/student/communication-support/communication#search-filter-and-sort-messages).

When the search is scoped to a single course, it also offers that course's own pages. Typing **grades**,
**exercises** or **tutorials**, in English or German, offers the matching page of the course, if the course has it.

## Narrowing the Results with Filters

Open the filter picker with **Filter** next to the search field, or with **Cmd+F** / **Ctrl+F**:

- **Filter by type** shows only one kind of result: **Courses**, **Exercises**, **Lectures**, **Slides & Videos**,
  **Channels**, **FAQs** or **Exams**.
- **Scope to a course** searches only one of your courses. Running courses are listed first.
- **Exclude content** hides a kind of result (**A type**) or a course (**A course**).

You can also type a filter straight into the search field:

| Type | Effect |
|------|--------|
| `type:` | Shows only one kind of result |
| `course:` | Searches only one course |
| `-type:` | Hides one kind of result |
| `-course:` | Hides one course |

As soon as you type the colon, the filter picker lists the values that fit what you type after it. Press **Tab** or
**Enter** to apply the highlighted value. If nothing matches, for example because the colon belongs to your search
term, you can search for the text as you typed it instead. Your search term stays where it is while you add a filter,
so you can filter after typing.

{/* SCREENSHOT type-values.png: "type:" typed into the search field, the operator highlighted in colour, and the value
    list with Courses, Exercises, Lectures, Slides & Videos, Channels, FAQs and Exams with their descriptions, one row
    highlighted with the Tab hint. Caption: "Choosing a type after typing type:". */}

Each filter appears as a chip under the search field. You can add several, for example two courses and two kinds of
result; a hiding filter shows its value struck through. Click a chip to change it, or its **×** to remove it. With the
cursor at the start of the search field, **Backspace** selects the last chip and a second **Backspace** removes it;
the arrow keys move between the chips.

{/* SCREENSHOT chips.png: a search term with three chips under the field: a type chip, a course chip and a struck-through
    exclude chip, and the filtered results below. Caption: "Including a type and a course, and hiding another course". */}

A filter works without a search term as well: **Exercises** alone lists the exercises of your courses. At least one
kind of result always stays included, so you cannot hide every type at once. **Slides & Videos** cannot be combined
with the other types, because it searches inside the lecture material rather than through the list of lectures,
exercises and courses.

## Searching Slides and Videos

**Slides & Videos** searches the content of lecture slides and the transcripts of lecture videos, so it finds a topic
even when no lecture or unit is named after it. Each result names the course, the lecture and the lecture unit, shows
the slide page or the position in the video, and opens exactly there.

You do not need to select the filter to see these hits. When your search matches lecture content, a row such as
**Slides & videos: 4 hits in Lecture A, Lecture B** appears above the results. **Show** switches to those hits.

Searching slides and videos uses AI models, so it is only available if you allowed AI in Artemis. See
[Choosing Whether to Use AI](/student/learning-content/iris#choosing-whether-to-use-ai).

## Asking Iris

If you allowed AI in Artemis and type a question rather than a name, for example *When is the deadline of the
second homework?* or *What is the difference between a stack and a queue?*, Iris answers it in a card above the
results. While it works, the card says what it is doing, such as **Searching course material…** or
**Found material in** followed by the courses it found something in. The answer then appears word by word.

{/* SCREENSHOT iris-thinking.png (optional): the Iris card while it works, showing the status "Found material in" with
    a course name, the results list already visible below. Caption: "Iris looking through the course material". */}

Iris answers only from your course material: lecture slides and video transcripts, and the course information that
global search finds, such as exercises with their due dates and points, exams, FAQs and channels. It only uses what you
are allowed to see, and only courses in which your instructor has Iris enabled. It answers in the language of your
question, even when the material is in another language. When the material does not answer a question but names a
lecture or exercise about it, Iris points you to that material instead.

Iris does not answer every search. A term that is short or reads like a name, such as a lecture title, gets the
results list only. If Iris looks for material but finds nothing relevant enough, the card says so briefly and then
closes; the results below are unaffected. If something goes wrong, **Try again** asks once more.

  An answer from Iris is generated by an AI model and can be wrong. Check important details, such as a deadline, in
  the source the answer cites.

</Callout>

### Citations

Every statement in an answer ends with a small number that refers to the source it comes from. The sources are listed
below the answer as chips with the same numbers; when an answer cites more than two lecture sources, a chip such as
**+3 more** shows the rest. Hovering over
a number highlights its sources and shows a preview; hovering over a source highlights the statements it supports.
Clicking either opens the source: the slide page or the video moment of a lecture source, or the page of an exercise,
exam, FAQ or channel. Long answers are shortened; **Show more** shows the rest.

### Continuing in the Course Chat

A finished answer ends with **Have a follow-up? Continue chatting with me about** followed by the lecture, exercise or
course that the answer cites most. **Open chat**, or **Cmd+Enter** / **Ctrl+Enter**, opens the course's Iris chat in a
new conversation on that lecture, exercise or course, with your question and the answer already in it, so you can ask
your follow-up question directly. The citations in the chat open the same slides and video moments as in the search.
The conversation is named after your question, so you find it again in your chat history. See
[Iris, the AI Tutor](/student/learning-content/iris).

## Keyboard Shortcuts

| Keys | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Cmd+K** / **Ctrl+K** | Open or close the search |
| **↑** / **↓**, **Enter** | Move through the results and open the selected one |
| **Cmd+F** / **Ctrl+F** | Open the filter picker |
| **Tab** | Apply the highlighted filter value |
| **Backspace** at the start of the field | Select the last filter chip, then remove it |
| **Cmd+Enter** / **Ctrl+Enter** | Continue the Iris answer in the course chat |
| **Esc** | Go back one step, or close the search |
