## Your Summary

Handing in takes you to your summary, and it stays available afterwards — reach it the way you reached the exam, or
pick the exam from the course sidebar once it is over.

The summary collects everything you submitted. For a programming exercise it also records the commit hash and the
repository address, so you can go back and read your own code. **Export PDF** saves the whole thing, which is worth
doing for your own records.

<Image src={examSummary} alt="Exam summary before results are published" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Your summary before results are published" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Your instructor may set a date before which the summary is not available. This is normal for exams written in
  several shifts, so that the content does not reach a later shift.

</Callout>

## Results and Complaints

When results are published, your score appears in the same summary.

If you think a manually assessed exercise was marked wrongly, and you are inside the review window your instructor
set, **Complain** lets you say why. A different assessor from the one who marked your work reviews the complaint and
answers it, and the answer appears in your summary. A successful complaint updates your result automatically.

Automatically assessed exercises cannot be complained about, unless an assessor has reviewed the automatic result by
hand — then that review is open to complaint like any other.

<Image src={complaint} alt="Complaining about the assessment of a text exercise" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Filing a complaint" />

A later **Export PDF** includes your score, your complaints and the responses to them.

## AI Feedback in Test Exams

A **test exam** is a practice run, so it is the one place in exam mode where you can ask for AI feedback on your
answers. Once you have submitted a test exam, its summary offers **Request AI Feedback**. One request covers all
text and modeling exercises of that exam at once, and the results appear in the summary itself a short while later
— you do not have to navigate anywhere.

The button shows how many requests you have used out of your limit, and turns into **AI Feedback Requested** — with
a spinner — as soon as Artemis accepts the request, until the feedback is ready. The first time you use it, Artemis
asks you to agree to sending your submission to an external AI service.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  The action appears only for submitted test exams, only while your Artemis instance runs the Athena service, and
  only if at least one exercise in the exam is configured for AI feedback. Real exams never offer it.

</Callout>

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  AI feedback in a test exam is preliminary and never a grade. It exists to help you prepare, not to predict your
  result in the real exam.

</Callout>

## Example Solutions

If your instructor set a publication date for them, example solutions appear after that date for text, modeling,
file upload and programming exercises.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  Example solutions are only available if you submitted your exam.

## Grades

Your summary shows the points you achieved and the maximum available for each exercise. If your instructor defined
a grading key, it also shows your grade — passing grades in green, failing grades in red.

<Image src={studentGrade} alt="Exam grade shown in the summary" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Your grade in the summary" />

Where a bonus is configured, your final grade with the bonus applied appears below your grade before the bonus.

<Image src={studentGradeWithBonus} alt="Exam grade with a bonus applied" size={ImageSize.large} caption="A grade with a bonus applied" />

**Grading Key** shows every grade step with its exact boundaries. The brackets say whether a boundary belongs to
the step: a square bracket includes it, a parenthesis excludes it. So a step written `[80 - 85)` means 80% earns
that grade and 85% earns the next one up.

<Image src={studentGradeKey} alt="Grading key showing every grade step and its boundaries" size={ImageSize.medium} caption="The grading key for a student who scored 135 of 150 points" />
