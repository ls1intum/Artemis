A text exercise asks you to write an answer in prose — an explanation, an argument, a comparison. You write it in
Artemis, in an editor beside the task, and a tutor reads and marks it after the due date.

## Start the Exercise

The exercise appears on the course's exercise list once it is released, with its due date and the points it is
worth. **Start exercise** creates your participation; until you do that there is nothing to write into.

<Image src={exerciseCard} alt="Text exercise before starting, with the task and the due date" size={ImageSize.large} caption="A text exercise before you start it" />

## Write and Submit

Once you have started, opening the exercise takes you straight to the editor: the task on one side, your answer
on the other, with a running word and character count above the box.

Nothing is stored while you type. **Submit** stores what you have written, and you can submit again as often as
you like before the due date — the last version is the one that is marked.

Leaving the exercise inside Artemis stores what is in the box as well, and closing the tab asks you first. Either
way, anything that reaches Artemis before the due date counts as your submission, so there is no such thing as a
draft you saved but forgot to hand in.

<Image src={textEditor} alt="Text editor with the answer box beside the task" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Writing an answer" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  After the due date nothing you write is counted any more. Submit before it passes, and do not rely on leaving the
  page to save the work for you.

</Callout>

## Your Result

Text answers are marked by a person, so nothing appears straight away. Once the assessment is published you see the
points, and the feedback attached to the parts of your answer it refers to.

Where your instructor has turned it on, you can ask for preliminary AI feedback on a draft before the due date. It
is a guide, not a grade: the mark that counts is the one a tutor writes afterward.
