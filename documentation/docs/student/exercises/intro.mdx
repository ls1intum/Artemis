# Exercises

## Overview

Artemis offers different exercise types to help you practice and demonstrate your knowledge. Each exercise type has its own characteristics and ways of submission.

### Exercise Types

Artemis supports the following exercise types:

- **Quiz Exercises**: Test your knowledge with multiple-choice, drag-and-drop, and short-answer questions
- **Modeling Exercises**: Create UML diagrams and other models using the integrated Apollon editor
- **Programming Exercises**: Write and submit code solutions with automated testing and feedback
- **Text Exercises**: Write free-text answers to open-ended questions
- **File Upload Exercises**: Submit files such as documents, images, or other content

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  All exercises share common properties such as release dates, due dates, and assessment due dates. Learn more about exercise organization in the <a href="/student/getting-started/general">General Information</a> section.

</Callout>

### Common Exercise Features

All exercises share these common features:

- **Release Date**: The date when the exercise becomes visible to you
- **Due Date**: The deadline for submitting your solution
- **Assessment Due Date**: The date when you can view feedback from instructors
- **Points**: Points you can earn, which may count toward your course score or serve as bonus points

Two features apply across exercise types:

- **Exercise Variants**: Your instructors can offer the same task in several equivalent versions. They appear as one entry in your exercise list, and you choose which version to work on. See <a href="/student/exercises/exercise-variants">Exercise Variants</a>.
- **Practice Mode**: After the due date you can keep working on an exercise without affecting your grade. See <a href="/student/exercises/practice-mode">Practice Mode</a>.

## The Exercise View

Whatever the type, an exercise opens in one view that keeps everything in one place — you no longer move between separate pages to start, submit and look at results.

- The **work area** holds what you do: the code editor, the modeling canvas, the text editor, the quiz questions or the upload form.
- Beside it, panels give you the **problem statement**, the course **communication** feed, and the Iris tutor where your course offers it. Drag the divider to give either side more room.
- The **header** stays on top with the exercise title, information tiles (your points, the deadline, your status, the difficulty) and every action you can take right now: start, submit, request AI feedback, or get the repository URL.

### Your Results

The status tile shows your latest result. Click the arrow next to it to open the **result history** and see all your earlier results for this exercise, newest first. Selecting one opens the submission it belongs to, so you can compare attempts without leaving the exercise.

<Image src={resultHistory} alt="Result history dropdown in the exercise header" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The result history lists every result of the exercise" />

Results earned in practice mode are labelled **Practice**, and AI-generated feedback is marked with its own icon, so you can always tell what counted towards your grade.

### Getting Started

Select an exercise type from the sidebar to learn more about how to participate and submit your solutions.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  Make sure to submit your solutions before the due date. Late submissions may not be accepted depending on your instructor's settings.

</Callout>
