A modeling exercise asks you to draw rather than to write: a UML class diagram, an activity diagram, a use case
diagram. You build it in Artemis's own editor, and it is marked element by element — a comment and a mark against
the class or the association it concerns, rather than one mark for the whole picture.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  <strong>Note:</strong> Artemis uses an npm package called <a href="https://www.npmjs.com/package/@tumaet/apollon">Apollon</a> as its modeling editor. It has a standalone version which can be accessed via <a href="https://apollon.ase.in.tum.de/">apollon.ase.in.tum.de</a>. The standalone version is free to use without the necessity of creating an account and offers additional features, including sharing and exporting diagrams. For more information, visit <a href="https://github.com/ls1intum/Apollon_standalone">Apollon Standalone</a>.

</Callout>

## Start the Exercise

The exercise appears on the course's exercise list once it is released, with the task, the points it is worth and
its due date. **Start exercise** creates your participation; until you do that there is no canvas to draw on.

<Image src={modelingExerciseCardStudentView} alt="Modeling exercise before starting, with the task and the due date" size={ImageSize.large} caption="A modeling exercise before you start it" />

Once you have started, opening the exercise takes you straight back to the editor.

## Draw and Submit

The task sits beside the canvas, and the components your diagram type offers are in the palette on the left: drag
one onto the canvas, then drag from its edge to another element to connect them. **Explanation**, under the canvas,
is worth writing whenever a choice in the diagram needs justifying — an assessor reads it alongside the diagram.

The editor saves the diagram by itself, roughly every thirty seconds while there is anything unsaved, and each of
those saves counts as your submission as long as the due date has not passed. **Submit** saves straight away and
confirms it, and it refuses an empty diagram, which the automatic save does not check. You can submit as often as
you like before the due date — the last version is the one that is marked.

<Image src={modelingExerciseStudentsInterface} alt="Modeling editor with the palette, the canvas and the task beside it" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Drawing a diagram" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Because the automatic save is already a submission, a diagram left in the editor before the due date is handed
  in. After the due date it is not: the editor still saves, but nothing saved then is counted.

</Callout>

## AI Feedback on a Draft

Where your instructor has switched it on, **Request AI feedback** gives you preliminary feedback on a submitted
diagram before the due date, so you can fix what it finds and submit again. It reads both the **structure** of your
diagram and its **layout**, and the comments land on the individual elements they are about rather than arriving as
one verdict on the whole thing.

It is not your mark: a tutor or instructor assesses your work after the due date regardless.

See [AI Feedback on a Draft](/student/exercises/ai-feedback) for how to ask for it, what comes back, and the limits
on how often.
