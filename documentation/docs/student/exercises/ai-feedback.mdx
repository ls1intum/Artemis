Where your instructor has switched it on, you can ask Artemis for feedback on a draft before the due date, without
waiting for a tutor to get to it. You can then act on it and submit again.

**It is not your mark.** The score it shows is provisional, it does not count towards your grade, and a person marks
your work afterwards regardless. Artemis says so wherever this feedback appears.

## Asking for It

**Request AI feedback** sits beside the exercise, on the exercise page and inside the editor. Submit your work
first, then press it.

<Image src={requestEditor} alt="The Request AI feedback button beside a modeling exercise in the editor" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Asking for feedback on a submitted draft" />

The button only appears when three things are true: your instructor has turned the feature on for the course, your
administrator has an AI service configured, and the exercise is not part of an exam. If you cannot see it, one of
those is missing — there is nothing to switch on at your end.

Which exercises offer it depends on the type:

| Exercise type | Offers AI feedback |
|---|---|
| Text | Yes |
| Modeling | Yes |
| Programming | Only where the exercise is assessed semi-automatically, meaning a person marks it as well as the tests |
| Quiz, file upload | No |

## What Comes Back

The request is not instant. Artemis takes a moment, and tells you when the feedback is ready rather than making you
watch for it.

<Image src={requestAlert} alt="A notification at the top of the page saying the feedback is ready" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Artemis tells you when the feedback has arrived" />

A provisional score appears with your submission.

<Image src={requestScore} alt="A preliminary score shown in the corner of the exercise page" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The provisional score — not your grade" />

Opening it shows the feedback itself, attached to the parts of your work it is about. On a modeling exercise that
means comments on individual elements of the diagram; on a text exercise, on passages of your answer.

<Image src={viewDetailed} alt="Detailed feedback shown inline against elements of a diagram" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Feedback attached to what it refers to" />

Every request is kept, so you can compare what you were told earlier against what you were told after your latest
changes.

<Image src={requestHistory} alt="The submission history, listing each feedback request" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Earlier requests stay available" />

## What You Get and What You Do Not

**Ten requests per exercise.** After that, **Request AI feedback** stops working for that exercise and Artemis says
you have reached the maximum.

**One request per submission**, on text and modeling exercises. Asking twice for the same submission is refused —
change something and submit again first. On a programming exercise the equivalent is a push: the feedback is about
the code Artemis has.

**Nothing after the due date.** The point of this feedback is to act on it while you still can, so the button stops
working once the deadline passes.

**No empty submissions.** There has to be something to give feedback on.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  You cannot complain about AI feedback. Complaints are about the mark a person gave you, and this is not that — see
  [Assessment and Feedback](/student/assessment-grades/assessment#complaints). If the AI feedback looks wrong, treat
  it as one opinion on a draft and use your own judgement.

</Callout>

## Making It Read the Way You Want

How detailed and how formal this feedback is are yours to set, under **User Settings → Learner Profile**. An
onboarding wizard shows you examples from each end of both scales so the choice is not abstract, and you can change
it whenever you like.

See [Adaptive Learning](/student/progress-analytics/adaptive-learning#feedback-learner-profile) for what each
setting does.
