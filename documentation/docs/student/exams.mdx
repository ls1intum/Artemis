Artemis runs exams in the browser. You work through the exercises inside a fixed window of time, hand in, and later
see your results and your grade in the same place.

## Before You Start

You need a stable internet connection — a wired one if you have the choice — and a current browser. Chromium-based
browsers such as Chrome and Edge get the most testing.

If your exam contains programming exercises, check with your instructor whether you are expected to work in the
browser, in your own editor, or either. If you may use your own machine you need the toolchain for the exercise's
language installed and working *before* the exam, along with a Git client. Most editors have one built in. Setting
up repository access, including SSH keys, is described under
[Integrated Code Lifecycle](/student/tools-reference/integrated-code-lifecycle) — do it in advance, not on the day.

## What Happens If Your Connection Drops

Artemis tolerates a short outage. Text, quiz and modeling exercises keep working while you are offline, though you
may see warnings that your work cannot be saved; when the connection returns, Artemis saves it. Your work is saved
every 30 seconds and whenever you move between exercises.

Programming exercises are different, because they need the server. In the online editor you have to submit
explicitly, and if you work in your own editor you need a connection to clone at the start and to push at the end.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.danger}>

  You must be online and submitted before the working time plus the grace period runs out. An exam that is never
  submitted is not graded.

</Callout>

## Two Rules Worth Following

**Do not reload the page.** Reloading returns you to the welcome screen, where you have to enter your name and
confirm the rules again. Reload only if something has broken in a way nothing else fixes.

**Work in one tab.** Two tabs on the same exam cause your work to be saved out of order, and the pattern is
recorded as suspicious behaviour that your instructors will look into. Other Artemis tabs are fine, as long as only
one of them has the exam open.
