# Deleting User Accounts

Artemis distinguishes two ways of taking an account out of service. **Deactivation** keeps the account and everything
attached to it, but refuses every way of signing in. **Permanent deletion** removes the account row and the data that
belongs to it, and cannot be undone.

This page describes what each of them does, how the deletion dialog in **Server Administration → User Management**
works, and which accounts Artemis refuses to delete. For how accounts are created and how the `activated` flag is
enforced, see [User Management](user-registration.mdx).

## Deactivation

Deactivation is the reversible option, and usually the right one:

- The account can no longer authenticate through any provider (password, LDAP, SAML2, OIDC, passkey) and neither over
  git via HTTPS nor via SSH.
- Its credentials are revoked: passkeys, SSH keys, and personal as well as participation VCS access tokens. Any
  outstanding activation or password-reset link is invalidated too, so a mail sent before the deactivation is no longer
  a way back in.
- Nothing else is touched. Submissions, results, exam records, and forum posts stay exactly as they are, and the
  person's name still appears wherever it did before.

Only an administrator can activate the account again. The revoked credentials have to be enrolled anew afterwards,
since they are gone rather than suspended.

:::note
A session that was already signed in keeps its token until it expires, because the token is validated from its own
claims. It cannot be renewed, so access ends when the token runs out.
:::

## Permanent deletion by an administrator

Permanent deletion is available in **Server Administration → User Management**, either from the row of a single account
or from the toolbar after selecting several accounts. It always goes through a preview and a typed confirmation.

### 1. Review the impact preview

Artemis counts, per account, everything that references it and groups the result by data category and by what will
happen to it:

| Action in the dialog | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Delete** | The record belongs to this account alone and is removed. |
| **Remove membership** | The record stays and the account is removed from it, for example a course, an organization, a team, a conversation, or a tutorial group. |
| **Keep record without user reference** | The record belongs to someone else and is kept, with the reference to this account cleared. This covers the work the account did *for* others, such as an assessment it graded, a complaint response it wrote, or a post it verified. |

The dialog also reports a total. That total counts the directly referenced records only. Everything hanging below
them, such as the replies under a deleted thread or the results under a deleted participation, goes with them and is
not counted separately, so the real number of removed rows is higher than the number shown.

:::caution Retention override
If an account still holds data that a retention period would normally keep, the dialog says so explicitly and marks
the affected accounts. Confirming the dialog then deletes that data ahead of its retention deadline. Consider whether
[deactivation](#deactivation) is sufficient, or whether the data should be left to the retention cleanup described in
[Cleanup Service](cleanup-service.mdx).
:::

### 2. Confirm

Type the confirmation the dialog asks for: the **login** when deleting a single account, or the **number of selected
accounts** when deleting several. The dialog also offers **Deactivate instead**, which deactivates the same selection
and leaves all data in place.

The preview is bound to what was counted. If the referenced data changes between opening the dialog and confirming it,
for instance because the student submitted something in the meantime, that account is not deleted. Artemis reports the
changed plan and reopens the preview with the current numbers so you confirm what is actually about to happen.

### 3. What deletion does

In order:

1. The account is deactivated and its course memberships are dropped, so it can no longer reach anything while the
   deletion runs.
2. Its credentials are revoked, and references on records belonging to other people are detached.
3. Data owned exclusively by the account is deleted: participations, submissions and results, exam registrations and
   sittings, complaints it filed, plagiarism cases against it, its posts, answers and reactions, its tutor
   participations, learning progress, Iris conversations, LTI launches, and its profile picture and data exports.
4. Teams the account owned are handed over to a remaining member, so the shared submissions and results stay
   available to the rest of the team. A team the account was the only member of is deleted with its work.
5. The account row itself is removed.

If Artemis encounters a reference it has no rule for, it aborts instead of leaving the account half-deleted. Nothing is
removed in that case, and the account remains as it was.

### Accounts that cannot be deleted

Permanent deletion is refused for:

- administrator and super-administrator accounts,
- bot accounts, including the Iris bot,
- the account configured as `artemis.user-management.internal-admin.username`,
- the account you are signed in with.

To delete a colleague's administrator account, first remove the administrator role through the user edit form, then
delete the account.

## Automatic deletion

Two mechanisms delete accounts without an administrator confirming anything.

**Unactivated self-registrations.** An account created through self-registration that is not activated within
`artemis.user-management.registration.cleanup-time-minutes` (60 by default) is deleted. This applies only to
self-registration; no other provisioning path schedules such a cleanup.

**Not-enrolled users.** Accounts that are enrolled in no course and have been inactive beyond the configured period are
first warned by email and then deleted after a grace period, provided they are still not enrolled and have not signed
in since the warning. Logging in cancels the deletion. This runs only if the corresponding cleanup jobs are enabled;
see [Cleanup Service](cleanup-service.mdx) for the schedules, cutoffs, and the safeguards around them.

Automatic deletion never overrides a retention period. An account is deleted only once every business-domain reference
to it is gone, and the owning retention cleanup is what removes those. An account that still has course roles,
participations, exam records, scores, complaints, plagiarism cases, posts, team memberships, tutorial-group
registrations, LTI launches, or course requests is therefore left untouched. The **Data Cleanup** page reports these
accounts as *Users blocked by remaining references*, and running the job manually does not override the check. Such an
account is deleted on a later run, once the applicable course, exam, plagiarism, or communication retention cleanup has
removed the data that holds it back. To remove it immediately, use the administrator deletion described above and
accept the retention override.

## Accounts left over from older versions

Artemis versions before the permanent deletion described here did not delete accounts, they anonymized them: the
personal fields were overwritten and the account was flagged as deleted, so that records referencing it stayed intact.
Such accounts may still exist on an upgraded instance. In the user list they appear as *Deleted User* with a random
login and an email address ending in `@user.deleted`, they cannot sign in, and no new ones are created.

They are cleaned up on their own. The automatic cleanup removes each of them as soon as no business-domain data
references it, which happens as the retention cleanups do their work, so no administrator action is needed.

## Audit trail

Every permanent deletion writes a `USER_PERMANENTLY_DELETED` audit entry naming the administrator who performed it (or
`system` for an automatic deletion), the account affected, and how many records the confirmed impact covered. The entry
appears under the **Application** tab of **Server Administration → Audits**, where it is kept for the application
retention period (five years by default).

Deactivation and activation are recorded as `DEACTIVATE_USER` and `ACTIVATE_USER` under the **Security** tab. See
[User Management](user-registration.mdx#audit-log) for the full list of account audit events, and
[Cleanup Service](cleanup-service.mdx#audit-event-retention) for how long each log is retained.

## Troubleshooting

**The confirmation button stays disabled.**
The confirmation field has to match exactly: the login for a single account, the number of accounts for a selection.

**Artemis reports that the plan changed and reopens the dialog.**
The data referencing one of the accounts changed after the preview was calculated. Review the updated numbers and
confirm again. Accounts that were already deleted in that attempt are not shown again.

**The dialog says an account still has retained data.**
That account holds data whose retention period has not elapsed. Confirming deletes it early. Deactivate instead if that
is not intended.

**An account cannot be deleted at all.**
It is an administrator, a bot, the configured internal admin account, or the account you are signed in with. Remove the
administrator role first, or sign in with a different administrator account.

**The Data Cleanup page keeps reporting blocked users.**
Those accounts still have data that a retention period protects. They are deleted automatically once the owning cleanup
has removed it. Nothing is wrong, and running the job again will not change the outcome.
