Everything about an exam is managed from one place. Open your course, choose **Exams** in the sidebar, and you see
every exam in the course. **Create a new exam** opens the creation form; **Import an exam** copies one you or a
colleague has already built.

## Choose the Exam Mode

**Mode** is the first decision, and the only one you cannot change afterwards.

An **Exam** is the end-of-semester case. You set a fixed window, every student works inside it, and you assess the
submissions afterwards — manually, automatically, or both. Results appear on the date you choose.

A **Test Exam** is practice. Instead of one fixed window for everybody, you set a window within which each student
may start whenever they like, and the working time then runs from their own start. Students get the automated
assessment for programming and quiz exercises as soon as they submit, so no manual correction is needed. For a test
exam the date fields are labelled **Start of working window** and **End of working window** rather than start and
end of working time, because they bound when a student may begin rather than when everyone writes.

## Fill In the Creation Form

The form is divided into the sections below, in this order. Only the exam title and the three conduction dates are
mandatory; leave a date blank and that step of the exam simply does not happen, which the form says above the
assessment dates.

### Exam Configuration

**Exam Title** names the exam everywhere it appears. **Examiner**, **Module number** and **Course name** are
printed on the exam and on the score export, so fill them in as your examination office expects them.

**Exam with attendance check** marks the exam as one where supervisors verify who is sitting at each machine. It is
what the [Exam Participation Checker](/instructor/exams/participation-checker) app works against.

<Image src={examConfiguration} alt="Exam Configuration section of the exam creation form" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exam Configuration" />

### Exam Conduction

Three dates define when the exam happens, and all three are required:

- **Visible from** — students see the exam and can open its start page, but cannot reach the exercises yet.
- **Start of working time** — students may begin.
- **End of working time** — students may no longer work.

**Working Time (minutes)** is derived from the two working-time dates rather than entered directly; editing it
moves the end date. **Grace period (seconds)** is the extra time students get to hand in after the working time
ends, and defaults to 180 seconds. No new submissions are possible during the grace period — it exists so that
pressing submit at the last second still works.

<Image src={examConduction} alt="Exam Conduction section with the visible, start and end dates" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exam Conduction" />

### Exam Exercises

**Maximum number of points for exam** is the total the exam is worth. **Number of exercises in exam** must equal
the number of [exercise groups](/instructor/exams/add-exercises#exercise-groups) you intend the exam to have. You
can leave it empty while building the exam, but it has to be set before Artemis can
[generate the individual exams](/instructor/exams/prepare-and-test#generate-the-individual-exams).

**Randomize order of exercise groups** gives each student the exercises in a different order.

<Image src={examExercises} alt="Exam Exercises section with points and number of exercises" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exam Exercises" />

### Exam Assessment and Student Review

**Number of correction rounds in exam** set to two lets a second tutor assess each exam after the first, which is
described under [second correction](/instructor/exams/assess-an-exam#assessment-with-second-correction-round).

**Release Date of Results** is when students see their results.

**Submission Overview Visible From** controls when a student can open the summary of their own submitted exam — the
questions, their answers, and the PDF export. Leave it empty and the summary appears immediately after submission.
Setting it later matters for an exam written in several shifts, so that early submitters cannot pass the content on
to a later shift. It has to fall after the end of working time and no later than the release date of results.

**Begin of Student Review** and **End of Student Review** bound the window in which students may inspect their
assessed exam and object to it. See [Student Review and Complaints](/instructor/exams/review-and-complaints).

<Image src={examAssessment} alt="Exam Assessment and Student Review section" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exam Assessment and Student Review" />

### Exam Solutions

**Example Solution Publication Date** is when students who participated can see the example solutions of every
exercise in the exam. Leave it blank to publish no solutions at all.

### Exam Texts

**Exam start text** and **Exam end text** are shown on the start and end pages during
[conduction](/instructor/exams/conduct-an-exam). Use the start text for the rules of the exam and the end text for
what happens next.

**Exam start confirmation text** and **Exam end confirmation text** are the declarations a student signs with their
full name before starting and after finishing. Artemis ships defaults for both, so change them only if your
institution requires different wording.

## Import an Existing Exam

**Import an exam** lists every exam from the courses where you are an instructor. Choose one and Artemis copies its
configuration into a new exam in your course.

<Image src={examImportSelection} alt="List of exams available for import" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exams available for import" />

The import form is the creation form with everything except the dates already filled in, so review it as described
above and set the timeline for your own exam. You can also select which exercises come across with the exam; the
[exercise group import](/instructor/exams/add-exercises#import-exercise-groups) explains what is and is not copied.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Dates are deliberately not copied. An imported exam has no timeline until you give it one, which is also why it
  cannot accidentally become visible to students.

</Callout>
