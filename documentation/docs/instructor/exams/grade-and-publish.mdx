Points become grades through a grading key, and both become visible to students on the date you set. Neither step
is mandatory: an exam without a grading key simply reports points.

## Grading Key

**Grading Key** at the top of the exam's detail page turns exam scores into grades automatically, and students see
their grade from the **Release Date of Results** onwards. It also improves the statistics, since a grade
distribution says more than a score distribution.

**Generate default grade key** followed by **Save** gives you a working key immediately. **Add grade step** builds
one by hand instead, and **Detailed** editing mode exposes the exact bounds of each step.

Grade steps are percentages of the total obtainable score by default. Set **Maximum number of points for exam** and
Artemis shows the point equivalent of each step alongside the percentage.

<Image src={gradeKeyEditor} alt="Grading key editor with the default grade steps" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The default grading key" />

Two fields decide how the key behaves at the edges. **Inclusivity** settles which grade a score exactly on a
boundary receives. **Grade Type** chooses between the two kinds of key:

- **Grade** produces a final grade with names you choose. Set **First Passing Grade** so Artemis knows which steps
  count as a pass.
- **Bonus** produces bonus points instead, which contribute to the grade of another course or exam rather than
  standing on their own.

The import and export buttons save a key as CSV and load it into another course or exam, which is how a
department's standard key gets reused rather than retyped.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  Read the warnings at the bottom of the editor before saving. They are what tells you the key has a gap or an
  overlap, and a key with either produces grades nobody can defend.

</Callout>

## Publication of Results

**Release Date of Results**, set in the
[exam configuration](/instructor/exams/create-an-exam#exam-assessment-and-student-review), is when students can see
their results in their summary. It is usually the end of assessment, but nothing stops you choosing another moment.

## Exam Scores

**Scores** aggregates the exam: the spread of achieved scores, the average per exercise, and every individual
result. Average, median and standard deviation are given for the exam as a whole, and students' own difficulty
ratings per exercise are collected alongside them, which is the most useful input into next year's exam.

Two filters change what the figures are computed from — **only include submitted exams** and **only include
exercises with at least one non-empty submission**. Both exist because unsubmitted exams and undrawn exercises
distort an average badly:

- An unsubmitted exam is never assessed, so it appears as no score at all. Where a grading key exists, its student
  receives the no-participation grade described under [Grading](/instructor/assessment-grading/grading).
- An exercise can be in no individual exam at all, if Artemis happened to draw a different exercise from its group
  every time.

<Image src={examStatistics} alt="Exam scores page with distribution and per-exercise statistics" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The scores page of an exam" />

**Export** writes the scores as CSV, which is what goes into a university system such as
[TUM Online](https://campus.tum.de). Each row carries the student's name, username, email and registration number,
the exercises they were assigned, their score per exercise, overall exam points and score, grade before any bonus,
presentation score, and whether they submitted and passed. The file also carries the aggregated per-exercise
statistics — number of participations and average score.

Three things appear only when they apply: bonus grades and the final grade when a bonus is configured, plagiarism
verdicts when the exam has at least one, and plagiarism verdicts from the bonus source when that has one.

## Exam Solutions

**Example Solution Publication Date** publishes the example solutions of the text, modeling, file upload and
programming exercises to the students who submitted. Unlike course exercises, which each carry their own date, an
exam publishes all of its solutions together on this one.

Leave it empty — the default — and no solutions are ever published. If set, it must fall on or after both
**Visible from** and **End of working time**.
