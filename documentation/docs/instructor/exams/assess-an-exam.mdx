Assessment opens once the last student's working time has expired — the **Assessment** dashboard is inaccessible
before then. Programming exercises already carry their automatic results; everything else waits for your
correction team.

## Assessment

Two actions on the **Assessment** dashboard finish the automatic part of the grading, and neither happens on its
own:

- **Evaluate quizzes** grades every quiz submission in the exam. Skip it and the quizzes stay ungraded.
- **Assess unsubmitted exams** awards zero points for exams that were never submitted and for empty submissions.
  Skip it and those submissions sit in the dashboard as apparently outstanding work.

<Image src={assessmentDashboard} alt="Assessment dashboard showing progress per exercise" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The assessment dashboard of an exam" />

The dashboard is the correction team's view of the exam: how many submissions each exercise has, how many are
assessed, and how the complaints stand. Assessment is anonymised — assessors do not see who wrote what.

Quiz exercises do not appear here at all, because **Evaluate quizzes** grades them in one step.

## Assess Student Exams

**Exercise Dashboard** on an exercise opens its correction queue, and **Start new assessment** hands the assessor a
random unassessed submission. Where an exercise carries example submissions, assessors have to work through those
first — see [Add Exercises](/instructor/exams/add-exercises#add-exercises).

Programming exercises are graded by their tests. Where
[manual assessment](/instructor/exams/add-exercises#programming-exercises) is enabled, an assessor can review that
result and add feedback of their own on top of it.

<Image src={programmingAssessment} alt="Manual assessment of a programming submission" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Reviewing an automatic result by hand" />

## Assessment with Second Correction Round

Setting **Number of correction rounds in exam** to two lets a second assessor grade every exam after the first has.

<Image src={setSecondCorrectionExam} alt="Number of correction rounds set to two" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Enabling the second correction round" />

The dashboard then reports each round separately, alongside the complaints.

<Image src={secondCorrectionStatus} alt="Assessment dashboard showing both correction rounds" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Progress across two correction rounds" />

Whether tutors may work in the second round is a per-exercise switch you can flip at any time. Turning it off again
does not remove second assessments that already exist.

<Image src={examAssessmentDashboardSecondCorrection} alt="Per-exercise switch for the second correction round" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Allowing tutors into the second round" />

In the second round, **Start new assessment** appears again on the exercise dashboard. The second assessment starts
as a copy of the first one's feedback, which can be overridden and added to; it is saved as a separate result
rather than replacing the first. While reviewing, assessors can turn on a badge that marks which round each piece
of feedback came from — currently for text, modeling and file upload exercises.

Both rounds are reachable from the exam's **Exercise Groups**, then **Scores** on the exercise concerned.

<Image src={scoresTable} alt="Scores page listing results per student" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Results of an exercise across both rounds" />

## Suspicious Behavior Detection

The suspicious behaviour dashboard is the first correction step on the exam checklist. An exam session is a unique
combination of IP address, user agent, instance id, session token and browser fingerprint, recorded whenever a
student enters their exam — so a student who wrote from two machines, or two students who wrote from one, shows up
as a pattern rather than as a suspicion.

<Image src={suspiciousBehaviorExamChecklist} alt="Suspicious behaviour step on the exam checklist" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Reaching the dashboard from the checklist" />

Choose which criteria to apply, then **Analyze Sessions**. Where the analysis finds something,
**View Suspicious Sessions** shows the detail.

<Image src={analysisOptions} alt="Available suspicious behaviour analysis options" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Choosing the analysis criteria" />

<Image src={suspiciousBehaviorAnalysisResults} alt="Results of a suspicious behaviour analysis" size={ImageSize.large} caption="An analysis result" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  A flagged session is a coincidence until you have explained it. Two students on one IP address is normal in a
  computer lab behind a NAT, and a changed user agent is normal if a browser updated mid-exam. Treat the output as
  a list of things to look into.

</Callout>

The lower half of the dashboard summarises plagiarism detection for the exercises that support it: how many
potential results a detection run returned, and how many an instructor has since confirmed.
**View Results or Run Detection** opens the detection page of that exercise, and once at least one case is
confirmed, **View Cases** lists every confirmed case in the exam.

<Image src={plagiarismCaseOverview} alt="Overview of confirmed plagiarism cases" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Confirmed plagiarism cases of an exam" />

## Plagiarism Detection

Plagiarism detection compares every submission of one exercise against every other and flags the pairs whose
similarity exceeds a threshold you set. You then compare the flagged pairs side by side and decide. Confirmed and
rejected cases can be exported as CSV for whatever process your institution runs.

Detection is per exercise, so reach it through the exam's **Exercise Groups** and open the exercise, where
**Plagiarism** sits among its actions.

<Image src={plagiarism} alt="Plagiarism detection on a modeling exercise" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Plagiarism detection for one exercise" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  The mechanics of comparing, confirming and communicating a case are the same inside and outside an exam and are
  described under [Plagiarism Check](/instructor/assessment-grading/plagiarism-check).

</Callout>

## Exam Timeline

Artemis saves the state of a text, modeling or quiz submission every 30 seconds and whenever the student saves, so
a submitted exam has a history rather than just a final state. **Exam timeline**, on the detail page of a submitted
individual exam, replays it: a slider moves through the timestamps and a navigation bar switches between exercises.
For a programming exercise it shows the git difference between the selected submission and either the previous one
or the exercise template.

The button is disabled until the exam is submitted, with a tooltip saying so.

<Image src={examTimelineExample} alt="Exam timeline replaying a student's submissions" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The exam timeline of a submitted exam" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  This is the evidence to reach for when a student says their work disappeared, or when a suspicious session needs
  explaining: it shows what was saved and when.

</Callout>
