Once a course exists — from a [course request](/instructor/course-management/course-requests) or from an
administrator — **Settings** at the foot of the course sidebar opens everything you can change about it. The page is
one long form in four sections: general information, exercises and grading, additional course features, and student
enrollment.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Some settings only appear when the instance supports them. **Online Course** is shown only where LTI is enabled, and
  the Iris settings only where an Iris service is configured. A setting you cannot find is usually one your instance
  does not offer rather than one that has moved.

</Callout>

## General Information

<Image src={generalInformation} alt="General information section of the course settings" size={ImageSize.large} caption="General information" />

**Title** is the course name everywhere it appears — the dashboard, course headers, notifications. **Color** sets the
accent used throughout the course, which is what lets a student tell two courses apart at a glance.

**Short Name** is the identifier: 3 to 24 letters and digits, starting with a letter, used in repository names.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  The short name cannot be changed after the course is created. Everything named after it, such as every repository,
  keeps that name for the life of the course.

</Callout>

**Course Description** is what students read when they enroll and in the course header. **Start** and **End** bound
the course; exercises and enrollment are expected to fall inside that window, and the end date is also what makes the
course archivable.

**Test Course** marks a course as one used for trying things out. It is kept apart from the real ones rather than
hidden: a student enrolled in it sees it on their dashboard under **Current test courses**, separate from the courses
that count, and the same separation is made in their course archive.

**Semester** groups courses on the student dashboard — `WS25`, `SS26` — so a student's older courses fall away from
the current ones.

**Tutorial Group Time Zone** is the zone tutorial group sessions are scheduled in and displayed against.

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.warning}>

  Changing the time zone on a course that already has tutorial group sessions shifts the times those sessions display
  at. Check the schedule afterwards.

</Callout>

### Data Retention

Two settings decide how long Artemis keeps this course's student data, and they exist because the answer is a legal
one rather than a preference.

**Grade-relevant course** marks a course whose results count towards official grades or exam records. Its student
data is then kept for the legally required period — five years by default. Turn it off for a course that is not
grade-relevant and the data is deleted sooner, a year by default.

**Data-retention hold** suspends that deletion entirely. Turn it on while an objection or legal proceeding about the
course is pending, because a pending proceeding extends the retention period by law: a course on hold is never warned
about, and never included in, the automatic deletion of old student data. Turn it off once the matter is settled.

### Author of the First Commit

Artemis creates the first commit of a repository itself, and **Author of the first commit** decides who is shown as
its author, for example in the commit history of the repository:

- **The user who creates the exercise** is the default and the behavior of every course that never changed this
  setting. Nothing is rewritten: a repository keeps the author it was created with. A student repository keeps the
  author of the first commit of the template repository. That author can differ from the creator of the exercise if
  the course used another option when the exercise was created or imported.
- **The Artemis placeholder** uses the name and email address that the server configures as `artemis.git.name` and
  `artemis.git.email`, so no person appears as the author.
- **A name and email address of your choice** uses an identity you enter, for example a course team. Students see this
  address in the commit history, so use one that you are happy to show them. The name may have up to 100 characters and
  the address up to 254. A name must not contain `<` or `>` or line breaks, and the address must be a valid email address.

The setting applies to these repositories, after you saved it:

- the template, solution and test repository of a programming exercise that you create from scratch or import from a
  file. A file import carries no history, so its first commit is the import itself;
- the repository of every student, for each repository that Artemis creates from then on. This includes the exercises
  that existed before you chose, and exercises that were imported from another exercise. A student repository starts
  with one commit that copies the template, and the setting decides its author.

It does not change repositories that already exist, and it does not rewrite history. An exercise that you import from
another exercise keeps the history of its source, so its template, solution and test repository keep the author of the
first commit they were copied with. The auxiliary repositories keep their author as well. The setting is part of the
form of an existing course. It is not shown while a course is created, so it starts with the default.

## Exercises and Grading

<Image src={exercisesAndGrading} alt="Exercises and grading section, with the complaint settings" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Exercises and grading" />

**Max. Points for Course** is used for the example calculations in the [grading key](/instructor/assessment-grading/grading).
It does not itself decide anyone's grade — that comes from the exercise points and the key.

**Decimal Places for Score Calculations** is the precision scores are calculated to, from 0 to 5 places, and defaults
to 1.

**Default Programming Language** pre-selects the language on the programming exercise form. It is a convenience only
and can be changed per exercise.

### Complaints

**Enable Complaints** lets students dispute an assessment. It opens five further settings.

| Setting | Default | Effect |
|---------|---------|--------|
| **Max. Number of Complaints per Student** | 3 | How many complaints a student may have open or rejected at once |
| **Max. Number of Complaints per Team** | 3 | The same limit for a team |
| **Complaint Due Date in Days After Result Date** | 7 | How long after their result a student may complain |
| **Max. Number of Characters per Complaint** | 2000 | How much a student may write |
| **Max. Number of Characters per Response** | 2000 | How much the reviewer may write back |

An accepted complaint stops counting against the limit, so the cap only restrains students whose complaints are not
upheld. See [Assessment and Grading](/instructor/assessment-grading/assessment#complaints) for how a reviewer answers
one.

### More Feedback Requests

**Enable More Feedback Requests** lets a student ask for their marks to be explained rather than changed, with
**More feedback request due date in days after result date** setting the window, again seven days by default.

These requests do not count against the complaint limit and cannot change a score, which makes them the cheaper
option for a student who is confused rather than aggrieved. They are also exclusive with complaints — Artemis keeps
one or the other per result — so it is worth telling students which one they want before they choose.

## Additional Course Features

<Image src={additionalFeatures} alt="Additional course features, with the communication settings" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Additional course features" />

**Learning Paths Enabled** builds each student an individual path through the course's competencies and the material
linked to them. See [Adaptive Learning](/instructor/analytics/adaptive-learning) for defining those competencies.

**Communication Enabled** turns on course channels: staff create channels per topic, and students ask questions on
exercise and lecture pages. Enabling it opens two more settings.

**Direct Messages / Group Chats Enabled** allows private conversations, one to one or in groups of up to ten. With it
off, communication is limited to channels everyone can see.

**Code of Conduct** is the rules for messaging in the course, written in markdown. A newly created course starts from
a template, which is worth reading before you edit it — it is also what the iOS and Android clients show for a course
that has none of its own. Every participant sees whatever is in it.

See [Communication](/instructor/communication-support/communication) for managing and moderating channels.

## Student Course Enrollment

<Image src={studentEnrollment} alt="Student course enrollment section, with the enrollment period and the confirmation message" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Student enrollment" />

**Student Course Enrollment Enabled** lets students join the course themselves while it is active. It opens four
further settings.

**Enrollment Start** and **Enrollment End** bound the window in which students may join; left empty, they follow the
course's own start and end.

**Enrollment Confirmation Message** is markdown a student must confirm as they enroll — terms of participation, an
honor code, anything they should acknowledge before joining.

**Student Course Unenrollment Enabled** lets students leave again, and **Latest Date to Unenroll** is the last day
they may; left empty, it is the end of the course.

**Online Course** marks the course as one run through an external platform over LTI, so accounts are created
automatically for students arriving that way. It appears only when LTI is enabled on the instance and self-enrollment
is off, because the two are mutually exclusive. See
[LTI Configuration](/instructor/integrations/lti-configuration).

## Iris

Iris, the AI tutor, has its own settings page rather than a section on this form: **Iris** in the course management,
not **Settings**. One switch decides whether the whole course has Iris, and a **Level of Instructional Support**
decides how much it gives away.

See [Iris](/instructor/communication-support/iris) for those settings, and
[Iris for students](/student/learning-content/iris) for what a student meets — including the choice each student
makes for themselves about whether to use AI at all.

## AI-Assisted Feedback

Athena's AI feedback is not on this form either. The **Athena AI Feedback** panel on the course overview, next to
Iris, carries a single Enabled/Disabled toggle that switches both kinds of feedback together and takes effect as soon
as you set it — there is nothing to save. **Configure** opens the dedicated Athena settings page, where
**Formative feedback requests** and **Grading feedback suggestions** can be switched independently; **Athena** in the
course management sidebar opens the same page. The panel and the sidebar entry appear only where an administrator has
configured Athena for the instance. The general step of the course onboarding wizard
offers the same two independent switches.

**Formative feedback requests** lets students ask Athena for preliminary feedback on a draft *before* the due date. It
is feedback to learn from rather than a mark, and it does not replace the assessment that follows. With it off,
students in the course no longer see the button to request AI feedback.

**Grading feedback suggestions** offers Athena's suggestions to tutors while they mark submissions. A suggestion is a
starting point a person accepts, edits or ignores; nothing reaches a student without a reviewer submitting it. For
programming and text exercises, turning it on or off also starts or stops Athena's processing of the submissions at
each exercise's due date.

Both switches cover text, modeling and programming exercises, but a programming exercise only where it is assessed
semi-automatically, with manual assessment enabled alongside the tests. There is no per-exercise switch: every
exercise in the course follows these two, as far as its type supports them. See
[AI-Assisted Feedback](/instructor/assessment-grading/ai-feedback) for the details.

## Deleting a Course

Administrators delete a course on its **Settings** page. The confirmation dialog first lists what the deletion removes
with the course, such as users, exercises, exams, lectures, student work, posts and builds, and asks you to type the
course title.

The deletion runs in the background. After you confirm, a progress dialog shows the current step and how far the
deletion is. Large courses with many student repositories can take several minutes, and the request no longer has to
wait for them, so it does not time out. If you reload the page while the deletion runs, the progress dialog opens again.
When the deletion is completed, close the dialog to return to your courses. While a deletion, an archive or a reset of
the course is running, no other one of these can be started for it.

## Student Home

Students enter a course on [Home](/student/getting-started/courses#course-home). Use **Student Home: learning approach** to explain how students should approach this course, what learning through practice or discussion means here, and how to ask for support. This optional field accepts up to 2,000 characters. Clearing and saving it removes the guidance.

The guidance appears on a student's first Home visit. When [AI welcomes](/admin/course-home-ai) are configured and the student permits AI, it also informs contextual encouragement. Home otherwise uses deterministic messages. The teaching team is derived from active course instructors, editors and tutors, using their public names and profile pictures.

Home links to existing exercises, lectures, announcements, FAQs and communication threads. It respects publication dates, individual deadlines and course feature availability. Its progress counts submitted available tasks, counting interchangeable variants once; it does not claim that submissions establish mastery.
