Artemis has five exercise types — [programming](/instructor/exercises/programming-exercise/),
[quiz](/instructor/exercises/quiz-exercise), [modeling](/instructor/exercises/modeling-exercise),
[text](/instructor/exercises/text-exercise) and [file upload](/instructor/exercises/file-upload-exercise) — and they
have the same skeleton. What differs is what a student hands in and how it is marked.

## What Every Exercise Has

- **Release Date** — when the exercise becomes visible. An exercise without one is visible immediately.
- **Start Date** — when students may begin working. Without one, they may begin as soon as it is released.
- **Due Date** — when submission closes. Assessment cannot start before it has passed.
- **Assessment Due Date** — when the assessments are published. Everything marked before it is released at once on
  that date, so no student sees their result before the others.
- **Points** and **Bonus Points** — what the exercise is worth, and **Include this exercise in the course score
  calculation**, which decides whether those points count toward the course score, count as bonus, or not at all.

Exercises of the same topic can be bundled into a **variant group**, which gives them a shared timeline, lets them
share a points cap, and shows them to students as one entry. Artemis can also generate a variant of an existing
exercise with AI. See [Exercise Variants](/instructor/exercises/exercise-variants) and
[Generate Variants with AI](/instructor/exercises/variant-generation).

## Exercise Details for Course Staff

Select an exercise's title on the course management exercise list to see its type, title, statistics and saved
configuration. Section links scroll to information on the same page; they do not
hide the other sections, so browser search can find details throughout the page.
The exercise ID remains in **General**.

The action bar groups participation, statistics and assessment navigation separately
from editing and grading. **More actions** contains less frequent actions such as
plagiarism checks, export and exercise download, where supported. Programming
exercises keep **Version History** as a link beside the heading. Reset and delete
controls are in **Exercise administration** at the bottom, also reachable through the section navigation;
their confirmations remain required. Programming exercises offer **Edit in editor** directly in the action bar.

Before you confirm a deletion, the dialog lists what will be removed with the exercise, grouped by topic, for example
student participations, submissions and results, assessments and complaints, plagiarism comparisons, posts in the
exercise channel and, for programming exercises, builds. Large amounts are highlighted, so you notice an exercise
with a lot of student data before you delete it. Deleting an exercise runs in the background: after you confirm, a
progress dialog shows the current step and how many items are done. **Continue in background** closes the dialog
without stopping the deletion, and a message tells you when it finished or failed. The server keeps deleting even
if you close the browser tab. If you reload the detail page of a programming, text, modeling or file upload
exercise or of an exam that is still being deleted, the progress dialog opens again. When the deletion fails, the dialog or message shows the reason and the exercise
stays in the list; try again after fixing the cause. The same applies to deleting an exercise group or a whole
exam, where the progress also names the exercise that is being deleted.

**Create Variant** is a yellow action for supported programming and quiz exercises.
Its availability depends on your role and the server's Hyperion configuration; see
[Generate Variants with AI](/instructor/exercises/variant-generation).

Available actions depend on your role, the exercise's state, whether it belongs to a course or an exam, and the
server's enabled features. On narrow screens, some action labels collapse to icons with accessible names and
tooltips. The section links still scroll through the same visible details.

## Moving an Exercise Between Courses

The **Manage Exercises** dialog, which **Import** and **Export** on the course's exercise page open, moves exercises
of every type the same way:

- **Export**, which instructors see, offers the five exercise types. Pick one, then **Export** next to an exercise of
  the course to download it as a ZIP file. The file holds the exercise settings, the problem statement with its
  images, and whatever the type needs on top: the template, solution and test repositories of a programming exercise,
  or the questions and drag-and-drop images of a quiz. Student submissions are not included. The **Exports** feature
  toggle switches this off, see [Exports and archives](/instructor/course-management/exports#export-an-exercise).
- **Import** offers the same five types. Pick one, then either choose an exercise from any course you can edit, or
  switch to **From file** and upload a ZIP file that **Export** created, on this or another Artemis instance. The
  exercise editor then opens with the settings from the file, without dates, so you can review them before you save.

The exercise group page of an exam offers the same **Import** for each type.

## Telling Students About a Change

When you edit an exercise that is already released, write a short note in **What did you change?** at the bottom of
the edit form. Saving then notifies the students, editors and instructors of the course: the notification shows the
exercise title and your note, shortened after 255 characters. Leave the field empty to save without this notification.
In an exam, the same field reaches the students who are taking the exam, see
[Conduct an Exam](/instructor/exams/conduct-an-exam#updating-an-exercise-during-the-exam).

## How Students Find an Exercise

**Exercises** in the course navigation opens the course's exercise list. The sidebar groups the exercises by where
they are in time, so a student sees what needs doing before what is finished:

- **Future** — has not begun: its start date, or its release date when it has no start date, is still ahead. An
  exercise in this group stays here whatever its due date says.
- **Due Soon** — has begun and is due within three days.
- **Current** — has begun and is due more than three days from now.
- **Past** — the due date has passed.
- **No Date** — has no due date. An exercise that has begun belongs here even when it has a release date and a
  start date; it is the missing due date that decides.

A quiz is grouped a little more generously: one whose release, start or due date is today, or that is open now, or
that is released without a due date, counts as **Current**.

A group holding more than five exercises is split into weekly subsections, so a long-running course stays
navigable. Searching turns the subsections off and lists the matches flat.
