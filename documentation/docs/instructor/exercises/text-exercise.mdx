A text exercise asks students to write prose: an explanation, an argument, a comparison. There is nothing to run
against an answer, so every submission is read and marked by a person — though Artemis can suggest assessments for
passages that resemble ones already graded.

## Create the Exercise

**Create Text Exercise** sits on the course's **Exercises** page in course management. The form comes in three
parts.

The first part is what every exercise has: **Title**, **Categories**, **Difficulty**, and the **Mode** — individual
or team, which cannot be changed once the exercise exists. Then the four dates that shape the exercise's life:
**Release Date** makes it visible, **Start Date** lets students begin, **Due Date** stops them working, and
**Assessment Due Date** publishes the feedback. **Points** and **Bonus Points** set what it is worth, and
**Inclusion in course score calculation** decides whether it counts at all.

**Automatic assessment suggestions enabled** turns on the [Athena](https://github.com/ls1intum/edutelligence/tree/main/athena)
service for this exercise, which proposes feedback for text passages based on how comparable passages were graded
earlier in the same exercise.

<Image src={createTextExerciseForm1} alt="First part of the text exercise form, with title, dates and points" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Creating a text exercise: the first part of the form" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.danger}>

  Fields marked in red are mandatory.

</Callout>

The second part carries the exercise's content: the **Problem Statement** students read, an **Example Solution**,
and an **Example Solution Publication Date**. Leave that date empty and the example solution stays with the tutors
rather than reaching students.

<Image src={createTextExerciseForm2} alt="Second part of the form, with the problem statement and example solution" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The problem statement and the example solution" />

<Callout variant={CalloutVariant.info}>

  Most fields carry a hint. Hover the <Image src={hintIcon} alt="hint" inline /> icon beside one to read it.

</Callout>

<Image src={createExerciseFormHint} alt="A hint shown after hovering the icon beside a field" size={ImageSize.medium} caption="A field's hint" />

The third part links the exercise to the rest of the course. **Linked Competencies** attaches it to the
competencies you have defined — see [Adaptive Learning](/instructor/analytics/adaptive-learning).

**Assessment Instructions** are the grading rubric. Each instruction carries **Credits** worth of points, a
**Scale** naming the performance level it represents, a **Description** saying when to apply it, and **Feedback**
the student reads. **Limit** caps how often one instruction may contribute to a mark. Assessors apply them by
dragging one onto a passage, which is what keeps several people marking the same way.

<Image src={createTextExerciseForm3} alt="Third part of the form, with competencies and assessment instructions" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Competencies and assessment instructions" />

### Import an Existing Exercise

**Import Text Exercise** copies an exercise you already have instead of starting from an empty form. Pick one from
the list, and Artemis opens the creation form with every field filled in from it, ready to adjust.

<Image src={importTextExerciseModal} alt="Dialog listing the text exercises available to import" size={ImageSize.medium} caption="Choosing an exercise to import" />

## Manage the Exercise

The exercise appears on the course's exercise list, where each row offers the same set of actions: **Edit** reopens
the form, **Scores** shows what students achieved, **Participations** lists who took part, **Example Submissions**
manages the submissions assessors train on, and **Delete** removes it. The title opens the exercise itself.

<Image src={courseDashboardExerciseText} alt="Course exercise list with the actions available on a text exercise" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The exercise on the course's exercise list" />

## Assess Submissions

Assessment opens once the due date has passed. **Assessment** in the course navigation lists the exercises waiting
to be marked; **Exercise Dashboard** on the text exercise opens its own queue.

The first time you assess this exercise, Artemis shows the problem statement and a summary of the assessment
instructions and asks you to work through an example before starting. After that, **Start new assessment** hands
you one unassessed submission at a time, chosen at random so assessors cannot pick.

<Image src={exerciseAssessmentDashboard} alt="Exercise assessment dashboard with the option to start a new assessment" size={ImageSize.large} caption="The exercise's assessment queue" />

Click a text block to open the assessment dialog and give it points and feedback. To mark a passage Artemis has not
split out on its own, hold `alt`/`option` and click the first and last word of the passage you want. Dragging an
assessment instruction onto a passage applies its points and feedback in one go.

<Image src={assessmentModal} alt="Assessment dialog on a selected text block" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Assessing a text block" />

**Add new feedback** attaches feedback to the submission as a whole rather than to a passage.

<Image src={feedbackModal} alt="Feedback dialog for the submission as a whole" size={ImageSize.large} caption="Feedback on the whole submission" />

When you are done, **Submit** publishes the assessment, **Save** keeps it to finish later, **Cancel** discards it
and releases the lock so somebody else can take the submission, and **Exercise Dashboard** returns to the queue.

### Automatic Assessment Suggestions

Where the exercise has them enabled, Athena proposes feedback for passages resembling ones already graded in this
exercise. The suggestions are a starting point an assessor accepts, edits or ignores; nothing is published without
a person submitting it.
